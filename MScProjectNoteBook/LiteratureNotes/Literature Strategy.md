# **2. Recommended Databases (with rationale)**

Below are the **best databases** for your specific project, given its intersection of **healthcare operations, data architecture, analytics, HCI, and DSR**.

---

## **2.1 ScienceDirect (Elsevier)**

**Best for:**

- Healthcare operations research
- Clinical workflow studies
- Data warehousing and analytics in healthcare
- HCI and usability research

**Why it’s ideal:**

- Contains high‑quality peer‑review journals (e.g., _Journal of Biomedical Informatics_, _International Journal of Medical Informatics_, _Decision Support Systems_).
- Excellent for methodological papers and applied analytics.
- Strong coverage of health system performance, scheduling, and optimisation research.

---

## **2.2 IEEE Xplore**

**Best for:**

- **Technical architectures** (IDRs, ETL, interoperability)
- **Real‑time data processing** and health informatics platforms
- **NLP pipelines** (CogStack‑like systems)
- **Data models** (i2b2, OMOP implementations)
- **HCI engineering** and system usability design

**Why it’s ideal:**

- Focused on **engineering-grade** implementations: data streaming, warehouse architectures, clinical decision‑support systems, and algorithmic approaches.
- Strong for system architecture diagrams, integration methods, technical evaluation studies.
- Often includes NHS‑relevant informatics studies (especially in CogStack and Tesseract-related work).

---

## **2.3 Google Scholar**

**Best for:**

- **Breadth** — capturing grey literature and niche/unpublished institutional PDFs
- Quickly surfacing influential papers (high citation counts)
- NHS Trust-level research reports, theses, toolkits not indexed elsewhere
- Early access to preprints on IDRs or DSR

**Why it’s essential:**

- Broadest index — excellent for backward/forward citation searches.
- Helps identify _patterns and gaps_ missed in specialist databases.
- Useful when searching “CogStack NHS” or “NCWTMDS” where indexing in formal journals may be inconsistent.

---

## **2.4 PubMed / MEDLINE (Optional but Valuable)**

**Best for:**

- Clinical workflow papers
- MDT functioning and team communication research
- Time‑interval studies (diagnosis-to-treatment)
- Operational issues linked to treatment delays

**Why it helps:**

- Provides clinical grounding for operational findings.
- Useful for explaining _clinical context_ around MDT decisions, tumour site differences, etc.

# **3. Recommended Search Strategy Structure (for your dissertation)**

Your literature review search methodology can follow this structure:

### **3.1 Databases Used**

ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar, PubMed.

### **3.2 Date Range**

2010–2026 (captures contemporary IDR architectures, ISO 9241‑110:2020, CogStack 2018, NHS standard reforms).

### **3.3 Inclusion Criteria**

- Peer-reviewed articles
- National NHS guidance (official sources)
- Research on IDRs, clinical decision-support, operational dashboards
- Studies involving oncology pathways or hospital operations

### **3.4 Exclusion Criteria**

- Non-healthcare IDR papers unless architecture is transferable
- Pre‑2010 legacy architectures
- Studies not describing evaluation or design components

### **3.5 Screening Process**

1. Title/keyword screening
2. Abstract screening
3. Full‑text review
4. Data extraction into thematic categories (e.g., IDR architectures, MDT workflow, DSR frameworks)
