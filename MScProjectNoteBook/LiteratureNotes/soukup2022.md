> [!Abstract]
>
> Multidisciplinary teams (MDTs) are widely used in cancer care. Recent research points to logistical challenges impeding MDT decision-making and dissatisfaction among members. This study sought to identify different types of logistical issues and how they impacted team processes.This was a secondary analysis of a cross-sectional observational study. Three cancer MDTs (breast, colorectal, and gynaecological) were recruited from UK hospitals. Validated observational instruments were used to measure decision-making (Metrics of Observational Decision-making, MDT-MODe), communication (Bales' Interaction Process Analysis, Bales' IPA), and case complexity (Measure of Case Discussion Complexity, MeDiC), including logistical challenges (Measure of Case Discussion Complexity, MeDiC), across 822 case discussions from 30 videoed meetings. Descriptive analysis and paired samples t tests were used to identify and compare frequency of different types of logistical challenges, along with partial correlations, controlling for clinical complexity of cases, to understand how such issues related to the MDT decision-making and communication.A significantly higher frequency of administrative and process issues (affecting 30 per cent of cases) was seen compared with the frequency of equipment issues (affecting 5 per cent of cases; P &lt; 0.001) and the frequency of the attendance issues (affecting 16 per cent of cases; P &lt; 0.001). The frequency of the attendance issues was significantly higher than the frequency of equipment issues (P &lt; 0.001). Partial correlation analysis revealed that administrative and process issues, including attendance, were negatively correlated with quality of information (r = −0.15, P &lt; 0.001; r = −0.11, P &lt; 0.001), and equipment issues with the quality of contribution to meeting discussion (r = −0.14, P &lt; 0.001). More questioning and answering by MDT members was evident with the administrative and process issues (r = 0.21, P &lt; 0.001; r = 0.19, P &lt; 0.001). Some differences were observed in teams' socioemotional reactions to the administrative and process issues with the gynaecological MDT showing positive correlation with positive socioemotional reactions (r = 0.20, P &lt; 0.001), and the breast cancer MDT with negative socioemotional reactions (r = 0.17, P &lt; 0.001).Administrative and process issues were the most frequent logistical challenges for the studied teams. Where diagnostic results were unavailable, and inadequate patient details provided, the quality of decision-making was reduced.

## **Summary of the Study**

- Analysed **822 case discussions** across breast, colorectal and gynaecological MDTs.
- Identified **administrative, process, attendance, and equipment problems** that directly reduced:
    - Quality of information
    - Quality of contributions to discussion
    - Efficiency of MDT recommendations
- Administrative issues affected **30%** of cases, far more than equipment issues (5%) or attendance issues (16%).
- Such process failures undermined consistency and confidence in MDT outputs
## **Relevance**

- Demonstrates that MDTs generate **suboptimal or incomplete decisions** because of administrative and process failures.
- This directly contributes to **delays in the MDT → clinic → treatment pathway**, as unclear or incomplete MDT decisions affect booking, triage, and resource allocation.
- Underlines the need for **integrated operational data** to detect delays caused by administrative bottlenecks.

## **Critical Evaluation**

### **Strengths**

- Very large sample of MDT cases (n=822) improves reliability.
- Uses **validated observational tools** (MDT‑MODe, Bales IPA) to measure communication quality.
- Demonstrates statistically significant correlations between logistical issues and MDT decision quality.

### **Weaknesses**

- Cannot fully determine causality — observational design limits inference.
- Limited to three MDT types in UK hospitals; generalisability beyond these tumour groups is uncertain.
- Focuses heavily on **communication**, less on downstream operational metrics (clinic delays, breaches).

### **Implications**

This paper supports the assertion that **operational inefficiencies start at the MDT**, and propagate downstream — validating the need for your artefact to track post‑MDT movement in near‑real‑time.

## **Implications for Artefact**

- The artefact should **surface MDT outputs clearly**, with timestamps, decision categories, and completeness indicators.
- Dashboard must flag administrative/attendance‑based workflow gaps.
- Reinforces the need for **data lineage** and **MDT decision completeness metrics**.


## **Contribution to the Literature Gap**

- Shows MDT inefficiencies are well documented, but **no integrated system exists** to track how these inefficiencies affect **post‑MDT operational delays**.
- Validates the central thesis of  project.
